Privacy Policy — AnsNew PDF Reader

A desktop PDF reader that works entirely on your own computer.

Effective 10 October 2026 Applies to version 5.0.0 Publisher: ANSNEW TECH.

The short version

  • We do not collect any personal data. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no crash reporting, and no advertising.
  • The app makes no network connections. Your documents are opened, rendered, searched, filled in, printed and saved on your own machine.
  • It keeps your preferences on your own disk — a settings file and a session file under %APPDATA%\pdf-reader. Nothing is sent anywhere, and deleting those files removes everything the app remembers.
  • One button opens an external website. If you click Translate in the word-lookup popup, the selected word is put into a Google Translate link and opened in your own browser. That is the only time text leaves the app, and only because you asked for it.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy covers AnsNew PDF Reader, a desktop application for Windows, macOS and Linux, published by ANSNEW TECH. It explains what the application does with information on the device it runs on. It applies to the app itself, whichever way you obtained it — Microsoft Store, an installer, or a portable folder.

The policy does not cover any other software, website or service, and it does not cover the documents you open: those belong to you or to whoever gave them to you.

2. Data we do not collect

AnsNew PDF Reader does not collect, transmit, sell or share personal data. Specifically, it does not collect:

CategoryCollected?

There is no server operated by us for this application, and therefore no server that could receive data from it. There is no account to register, and no API key or subscription involved in any feature.

3. What is stored on your device

The app remembers a few things so it can reopen the way you left it. This information is written to your own user profile and is never transmitted:

FileWhat it holds
%APPDATA%\pdf-reader\settings.json Your preferences: theme, default view mode and zoom, whether the sidebar is shown, reading and lookup options, and the face, size, weight and colour used for text typed into forms.
%APPDATA%\pdf-reader\session.json The documents that were open when you last quit, and where you were in each one — page, scroll position, zoom and rotation — so they can be restored.

On macOS these files live under ~/Library/Application Support/pdf-reader; on Linux under $XDG_CONFIG_HOME/pdf-reader or ~/.config/pdf-reader. They contain no document content, only preferences and file locations.

If a document has unsaved changes and the settings file is ever damaged, the app preserves the damaged copy alongside it rather than overwriting it, so nothing is silently lost. That copy is still local to your machine.

4. Your documents

Documents are opened, decoded, rendered, searched and edited locally. Highlights, notes, form entries and page edits are written into the PDF file itself, and only when you choose to save — the app never writes to a file you did not ask it to change. Saving is done by writing a temporary file beside the original and then replacing it, so an interrupted save cannot leave a half-written document.

If you set AnsNew PDF Reader as the default application for PDF files, Windows may pass the path of a document to the app when you open it. The app uses that path to read the file and nothing more.

Exported images and printed pages are produced on your machine and handed to your own printer driver or saved where you choose. No document content is sent to us or to any third party by printing or exporting.

5. Network activity

The application does not make network connections. It contains no networking code, and the Windows package requests no network capability — the package manifest declares only runFullTrust, which is what a desktop application needs to run, and nothing related to the internet.

Everything the app does with a document is done locally: page rendering uses the copy of PDFium bundled inside the app, and word lookups are answered from dictionaries bundled with it. No feature requires an internet connection, and no feature is degraded when you are offline.

6. Third-party services

Google Translate — only when you click Translate

When you select a word and open the lookup popup, one of the buttons is Translate. Pressing it builds a link of the form https://translate.google.com/?sl=auto&tl=…&text=<your word> and opens it in your default web browser. The app itself sends nothing.

Because the selected word is part of that address, it is visible to your browser and to Google once the page loads, exactly as if you had typed it into Google Translate yourself. That exchange is governed by Google's own privacy policy, not by this one. If you never press Translate, no text ever leaves the app. The same is true of the word you look up: it is answered from the offline dictionaries and is not sent anywhere.

Links in the About window

The About window and the Help menu offer links to the developer's GitHub profile, personal website, ORCID record and Facebook page. These open in your browser only when you click them. Opening one is a normal web visit governed by that site's privacy policy; the app does not track whether you clicked.

Microsoft Store

If you installed the app from the Microsoft Store, Microsoft handles the download, installation and updates, and may collect information about that process under its own privacy statement. That happens between you and Microsoft and is outside the app: the app receives no personal data from it, and we receive no reports about individual users. The Store's optional ratings and reviews are likewise handled by Microsoft.

7. Local features explained

Word lookup and pronunciation

The English–Bangla dictionary, the pronunciation data and the English word list are files shipped inside the application. A lookup reads them from your disk and answers immediately. No query is sent anywhere, and the app keeps no history of the words you look up.

Speak

The Speak button pronounces a word using the text-to-speech voice already installed in your operating system. The word is passed to that local system voice; it is not sent over the internet, and nothing is recorded.

Search

Searching runs over the pages of the document you have open, on your machine. The query and the results stay in the app and are not stored after you close it.

Clipboard

Copying text places it on the system clipboard, which is how every application works. The app does not read the clipboard on its own.

8. Children's privacy

The app is a document reader suitable for general audiences. Because it collects no personal data from anyone, it collects no personal data from children, and there is no content, account or communication feature directed at children. Parents and guardians can let a child use it without a data-protection concern arising from the application itself.

9. Your control and how to delete data

You are in control of everything the app stores, because all of it is on your own device:

  • See it — open %APPDATA%\pdf-reader to read settings.json and session.json; both are plain, readable text.
  • Clear it — delete that folder. The app then starts with default settings and no remembered tabs. Settings also offers a Reset to defaults option that restores the original preferences.
  • Uninstall — removing the app through Windows Settings, the Microsoft Store, or the uninstaller removes the application. You may then delete the folder above to remove the leftover preferences.
  • Stop the one outbound action — simply do not press Translate. Every other feature works fully offline.

Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for you to request from us and nothing for us to delete on your behalf. There is no profile to close and no mailing list to leave.

10. Security

The app runs on your machine with your user account's permissions and does not expose a service, port or account that could be reached from outside it. Documents you open stay in your own storage; if a document is confidential, its confidentiality is protected by the same file permissions and disk encryption that protect the rest of your files.

Windows and macOS builds are released as signed packages. Where a package is signed with a self-signed certificate, the certificate must be trusted on the machine before the package can be installed — the package's integrity, not your data, is what that signature protects.

11. Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app starts doing something this policy does not describe — for example, adding an online feature — this page will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date at the top will change. Because the app stores no contact details, we cannot notify you individually; the current version of this policy always lives at this address.

This policy applies to version 5.0.0 of AnsNew PDF Reader. Later releases within the same behaviour remain covered by it.
